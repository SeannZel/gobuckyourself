How the values work

Where the numbers come from, how they're blended, and how the trade calculator uses them.

The GoBuckYourself value

Every player gets one number on a 0–10,000 scale, where the best player in a given format is 10,000. That number isn't a single site's opinion — it's a weighted blend of up to four independent views of a player's worth, nudged by a fourth signal for momentum. Because everything lands on the same scale, a quarterback, a tight end, and a two-for-one package can all be compared directly.

The sources and what each contributes

FantasyCalc market values (40%). Derived from thousands of real trades across leagues, so it reflects what managers actually pay, not what pundits think. Fetched separately for 1QB and Superflex and for PPR, half-PPR, and standard scoring, so no guesswork is needed to translate between formats.

ESPN average draft position (25%). The draft market: where players are being taken in real ESPN drafts, along with percent rostered. ADP is converted to the value scale with the same exponential curve that shapes trade values, so pick 1 ≈ 10,000 and value falls steeply through the first two rounds before flattening.

PeakedInHighSkool trade value chart (20%). A widely used weekly chart built from expert rankings, community data, and analytics, refreshed every Tuesday night. It's the "what does the fantasy community think this player is worth in a trade" leg, and often the first to move on news.

FantasyPros expert consensus (15%). The aggregated rankings of dozens of analysts, per scoring format. This is the "expert" leg that catches injuries and role changes before the trade market fully reprices them. (Requires an API key; if it's not configured the other weights are renormalized.)

Sleeper waiver momentum (±3% max). Net adds minus drops over the past week across Sleeper leagues. A player being scooped everywhere gets a small bump; one being cut everywhere gets a small haircut. It's capped so hype can't override the fundamentals.

How the blend works

For each format and scoring combination, every source's number is normalized to 0–10,000, then averaged using the weights above. If a source doesn't cover a player, its weight is dropped and the others are scaled up, so a deep-bench player known only to ADP still gets a sensible value. Superflex uplift for quarterbacks is learned from FantasyCalc's own 1QB-versus-Superflex ratio and applied to the ADP and consensus legs, rather than hard-coded. Finally the whole pool is rescaled so the top player is exactly 10,000, and tiers are assigned from the result.

The rankings page shows how many sources value each player and whether they agree, are mixed, or are split — a wide split usually means an injury or role question that the market and the experts read differently, which is exactly where trade edges live.

The trade calculator

The calculator sums each side's values, but not linearly. In redraft, roster spots are scarce and the best player in a deal usually decides it, so the second, third, and fourth pieces on a side are discounted (85%, 72%, 61% and so on). This is why a 3-for-1 that "adds up" on paper often still loses.

A trade is called fair when the adjusted totals are within your chosen tolerance (default ±6%). Otherwise the calculator names the side receiving more value and suggests players the winning side could add to balance the deal.

League sync & lineup impact

Connect a Sleeper league on the My League page and the site reads every roster straight from Sleeper, right in your browser (no login; Sleeper league data is public). It also switches the site to your league's QB format and scoring.

Lineup value. For each team we fill your league's actual starting slots (QB, RB, WR, TE, FLEX, superflex and so on) with the best available players, then add 20% of the top four bench players for depth. Power rankings sort teams by that number.

Trade finder. It tries every 1-for-1, 2-for-1 and 1-for-2 deal between you and each leaguemate, and keeps the ones that raise your lineup value by at least 1%, don't meaningfully hurt theirs, and are within 15% on trade value. Deals that help both lineups rank first, because those are the ones that get accepted.

Lineup impact in the calculator. When each side of a trade comes from one team in your league, the calculator shows how each team's starting lineup changes. That can disagree with the value verdict, and that's the point: a trade can "lose" on paper and still make your weekly lineup better.

Tiers

Tier 1 (8,000+) are league-winners you should almost never trade. Tier 2 (6,000–8,000) are weekly locks. Tier 3 (4,000–6,000) are strong starters. Tier 4 (2,500–4,000) are flex plays. Tier 5 and 6 are bench depth and streamers — useful as sweeteners, rarely as centerpieces.
